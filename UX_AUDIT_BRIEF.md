# Njiapanda UX Audit & Redesign Brief

## Audit Summary
The current Njiapanda homepage presents all features as equal-weight utility cards, resulting in a service menu effect. This approach is overwhelming, impersonal, and does not reflect trauma-informed design principles. Users may feel lost, judged, or pressured to act before they are emotionally ready. The interface lacks emotional guidance, reassurance, and clear signals of privacy or safety.

## Key Issues Identified
- Overwhelming cognitive load from too many equal options
- Absence of emotional validation or guidance
- No clear first step for users in distress or confusion
- Interface feels institutional, not human or safe
- Anonymity and emotional safety are not visually communicated

## Redesign Goals
- Emotional safety and reassurance as the foundation
- Recognition before action: help users name and process their experience
- Progressive disclosure: reveal options as users are ready
- Anonymity by default, always visible
- Calm, human, non-institutional visual direction
- Mobile-first, low-friction, accessible navigation

## Proposed Homepage Structure
1. Gentle welcome & emotional validation
2. Recognition & reflection (self-check tools)
3. Emotional support & connection (stories, resources)
4. Quietly explore options (progressive menu)
5. Take action only when ready (emergency/help tools)

## Feature Label Improvements
- "Quiet Help Signal" → "Signal for Help, Silently"
- "Share Your Story" → "Share, Anonymously"
- "Safety Planning" → "Plan for Safety"
- "Find Resources" → "Explore Support Near You"
- "Emergency Helpline" → "Talk to Someone Now"
- "Hadithi" → "Stories Like Mine"
- "Incident Tracker" → "My Private Notes"

## Onboarding & Interaction Flow
- Gentle welcome and privacy reassurance
- Emotional check-in (mood selector)
- Self-reflection tool
- Stories/resources
- Quiet reveal of options
- Action tools only after engagement

## Visual & Interaction Recommendations
- Large, soft typography; warm neutral colors
- Generous whitespace, single-column mobile layout
- Rounded, human-centric icons and cards
- Persistent, gentle anonymity/safety banner
- Sticky, non-alarming emergency exit
- Microcopy: "You are safe here.", "No one will know you visited.", "Take your time."

## What to Avoid
- No “report” or “form” on first screen
- No admin/dashboard links
- No institutional branding or colors
- No overwhelming service menu
